## Mission

SHBC champions the creation and inclusion of "gentle density housing" — laneway homes, garden suites, secondary suites, duplexes, triplexes, fourplexes, sixplexes, and small infill multiplexes — in established single-family neighbourhoods across BC. They define small housing as "just enough space to live."

The work spans three pillars:
- **Advocacy** — policy work with the Province of BC, municipalities, and CMHC
- **Research** — documenting what works, publishing toolkits, case studies, and bylaw guides
- **Direct enablement** — helping individual homeowners and builders actually deliver projects (consulting, training, design libraries)

## Why "gentle density"?

Their framing: single-family neighbourhoods make up roughly **80% of housing land** in a typical BC city. Adding 1–5 units per lot — without rebuilding the neighbourhood character — is the largest untapped affordability lever the province has. SHBC has been the loudest civil-society voice articulating this for over a decade, and the 2023–2025 provincial legislation (Bill 44, then Bill 25) is, in large part, the policy outcome they advocated for.

## History (compressed)

- **2005** — Jake Fry founds Smallworks Studios + Laneway Housing Inc. in Vancouver, working with the City to write the first laneway-housing zoning bylaws.
- **2012** — SHBC is founded as a non-profit society. Jake Fry is one of two founding directors.
- **2015** — Publishes first edition of the "Small Housing Toolkit" (PDF still hosted at `/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/SMHT_1stEdition_Feb2015.pdf`).
- **2017** — Pilot project: presents to 20+ BC municipalities, partners with three (Vancouver, Maple Ridge, Nelson) to drive small-housing uptake. Outputs: Maple Ridge laneway design lookbook, Nelson laneway how-to guide, Vancouver Permanently Affordable Homeownership feasibility study.
- **Nov 2021** — Wins CMHC Housing Supply Challenge "Getting Started" incubation funding, kicking off development of the Gentle Density Toolbox webtool.
- **2022** — Registers as a lobbyist organization in BC.
- **2023** — BC Bill 44 (SSMUH legislation) passes, mandating gentle density across the province. SHBC's policy work pays off.
- **Sept 2024** — Daniel Winer joins as Executive Lead.
- **Nov 2025** — Bill 25 passes, removing remaining barriers to SSMUH and setting a June 30, 2026 deadline for municipal bylaw updates.
- **2025** — Publishes the Gentle Density Housing Bylaw Guide (2025) and runs Multiplex Construction Training programs in Vancouver/Burnaby and Sea-to-Sky/Squamish.

## Structure (best public read)

SHBC is a **small, advocacy-led non-profit** — not a large operating org. The lobbyist registry lists Daniel Winer as the senior officer. There's a small team page on the site (`/team/`), with at minimum: Daniel Winer (Executive Lead), Tamara White (Executive Director / planner — title may have shifted late 2024), Akua Schatz (Chair / Director), Jake Fry (Co-founder Director), plus a David and likely others. Their model leans heavily on:

- **Project-based funding** (CMHC, REFBC, Vancity, municipal partners)
- **Consultant/contractor staff** for deliverables (e.g. Craig Keating registered as a Consultant Lobbyist)
- **Volunteer board / advocate network** (the Smallworks team and others in the small-housing industry)

This matters for the BuildOS conversation: they're not a 50-person product team. They're a credibility-and-content org with strong funder relationships. If they want a software product, they will almost certainly need to **partner** for the build, not staff it internally.
