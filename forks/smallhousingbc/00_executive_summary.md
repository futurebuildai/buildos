## The one-liner

Small Housing BC (SHBC) is a Vancouver-based non-profit society, founded in 2012, that advocates for and enables "gentle density" — laneway homes, ADUs, duplexes, triplexes, fourplexes, sixplexes, and infill multiplexes — in single-family neighbourhoods across British Columbia. They serve three audiences: homeowners (who want to add density to their lot), municipalities (who write the bylaws), and small-scale builders (who actually deliver the homes).

## Why this conversation is happening *now*

BC is in the middle of the most aggressive provincial upzoning in Canadian history. **Bill 44 (2023)** forced every BC municipality over 5,000 people to allow 3–4 units on every single-family lot, and 6 units near transit. **Bill 25 (Nov 2025)** removed remaining barriers and required all local-government bylaw updates by **June 30, 2026**. The "small housing" sector has effectively gone from a niche advocacy issue to the legislated default. SHBC is sitting on a decade of policy expertise, content, and credibility right when the entire province needs operational tooling — and they don't have it.

## The fit hypothesis (in one paragraph)

SHBC's current digital footprint is a **WordPress marketing site** plus a **content/resource library** at `toolbox.smallhousing.ca` (the "Gentle Density Toolbox"). They have *content* and *credibility* but no *operating system* for the homeowner-developer journey or for the small-scale multiplex builders they train. Meanwhile, FutureBuild's BuildOS is precisely an open-source operating system for residential builders, with AI document parsing, deterministic scheduling, and a project-mgmt backbone. **A BuildOS fork for SHBC could become the de-facto "How do I actually build a multiplex on my lot?" platform** for BC homeowners and the certified small-scale builder network SHBC is already training. They have the audience, the brand, the regulatory tailwind, and the funder relationships (CMHC, REFBC, Vancity, BC Housing) to underwrite it; FutureBuild has the product.

## The three big questions to answer on the call

1. **Who would the platform actually serve first** — homeowner-developers, the builders SHBC trains, or municipalities tracking gentle-density permits? Each is a different product wedge.
2. **Who pays?** A fork can be funded by a CMHC Housing Supply Challenge bid, by REFBC/Vancity, by a municipal procurement (Vancouver/Maple Ridge/Nelson are existing partners), or by SHBC raising capital directly. Need to find out which path Daniel envisions.
3. **What does "fork" mean to them?** White-label of BuildOS? Custom modules layered on the open-source core? Joint product with shared IP? Their answer will dictate scope and commercials.
