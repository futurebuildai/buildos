# Funding & Business Model

This is the most important section commercially. A "fork of BuildOS for SHBC" is a real-money product engagement; understanding *who would actually pay for it* shapes the entire commercial structure.

## The CMHC Housing Supply Challenge — most relevant funder for a BuildOS fork

This is the single most important funding context to understand for the call.

- The CMHC HSC is a multi-round federal funding mechanism for **innovative solutions to housing supply barriers**. Total program: ~$300M federally allocated.
- SHBC won a **Getting Started** (small-incubation) grant in Round 2 (~Nov 2021) to build the Toolbox.
- BC Housing won **$4M** from CMHC HSC to build the DASH platform — a much larger Implementation grant.
- CMHC has run **Rounds 1, 2, 3, 4 to date**, each with different themes (data, supply, missing middle, etc.).
- A "BuildOS fork for the SHBC homeowner-developer + small-builder ecosystem" is a textbook CMHC HSC pitch: open-source, scalable, addresses a documented supply barrier, has measurable outcomes.

**Strategic implication:** A fork doesn't have to be funded by SHBC's operating budget. The most natural commercial structure may be a **joint CMHC HSC bid** with SHBC as lead applicant and FutureBuild as the implementation partner. SHBC has won CMHC funding before, FutureBuild has the product. This is a much easier conversation than "SHBC writes you a check."

## Other funding paths for a fork

| Path | Who pays | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Joint CMHC HSC bid** | CMHC (federal) | Free money for both parties, public-good framing, SHBC has won before | Long timeline (12-18 months from RFP to award), competitive |
| **REFBC grant** | REFBC | SHBC has existing relationship, faster than CMHC | Smaller grant sizes (typically $50K–$250K), insufficient for full product build |
| **Municipal procurement** | A city (e.g. Vancouver) | Real revenue, fast if scoped to one customer | Single-customer dependency, RFP process |
| **Province of BC contract** | Ministry of Housing | Largest possible commercial scope; explicitly aligned with Bill 25 implementation | Long sales cycle; competitive with other vendors and DASH |
| **Vancity sponsorship** | Vancity Community Foundation | Aligned with their affordable-housing thesis | Scale-limited |
| **Self-funded by SHBC** | SHBC ops budget | Cleanest IP/control story | Unlikely they have the cash; would compete with their advocacy work |
| **FutureBuild eats it as R&D** | FutureBuild | Total IP control, rapid iteration | No revenue; only justified if SHBC is a strategic launch market |
| **Hybrid: FutureBuild builds it, SHBC distributes** | Builders + homeowners (SaaS) | Recurring revenue, scales | Requires consumer-grade product and SHBC operating as channel |

## SHBC's revenue side (what they sell)

The picture is partial here, but as best public read:

- **Consulting fees** — pre-design home reviews, development feasibility analyses, municipal advisory engagements
- **Training revenue** — Multiplex Construction Training (per-seat tuition or municipal-sponsored)
- **Speaking / honoraria** — leadership team is in-demand on the speaker circuit
- **Possibly small earned revenue from the Toolbox** — sponsorships, content licensing (unconfirmed)

There's no public evidence of a SaaS, subscription, or e-commerce revenue line. They are a grant-funded non-profit, not a self-sustaining product business.

## The commercial reality for a fork conversation

1. **Don't expect SHBC to write a $250K check.** They don't have it.
2. **Do expect SHBC to be a phenomenal funding co-applicant.** Their CMHC, REFBC, and Vancity relationships are the most valuable thing they bring to the table.
3. **The fastest path to revenue is probably the builder side, not the SHBC side.** If a BuildOS fork serves the small-multiplex builders SHBC trains, those builders pay for the SaaS, and SHBC gets a referral / channel cut or in-kind co-marketing. Lower commercial risk for both parties.
4. **The fastest path to scale is probably the municipal side.** A municipality buying a "gentle density permit-and-project tracker" for its planning department is a $50–250K/year deal that could be repeated across BC's 162 municipalities.
5. **The fastest path to brand is probably the homeowner side.** A free / freemium homeowner app keeps SHBC's mission alive (lowering the homeowner-developer drop-off rate) and is the most defensible long-term asset.

## Risks to flag

- **DASH already exists, well-funded, with a similar government backer** — must articulate a clean positioning gap (DASH = mid-rise prefab, SHBC fork = 1-6 unit lot-scale). Verify on the call that SHBC sees DASH as a peer/non-overlapping rather than as a competitor.
- **CMHC funding cycles are slow.** If SHBC needs the platform live by, say, the June 2026 bylaw deadline, grant funding probably can't deliver in time.
- **Smallworks–SHBC entanglement** is a governance risk. Anything FutureBuild builds for SHBC implicitly benefits Smallworks (Jake Fry's builder co.). Other BC builders may see this as conflict of interest. Worth raising delicately.
